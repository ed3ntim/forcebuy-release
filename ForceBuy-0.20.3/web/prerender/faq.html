ForceBuy FAQ

Short answers about what ForceBuy reads, what it stores and what it asks of you.

What is ForceBuy?

ForceBuy keeps a ledger of your CS2 items: what you own, what each item cost, what it is worth today and what you made on the items you sold. It reads your Steam inventory and your purchase and sale histories on the Steam Community Market, CSFloat and Buff163, and ties each price to the item it belongs to.

ForceBuy is an independent project and is not affiliated with Valve or Steam.

Where do the purchase prices come from?

From the histories of the places where you bought. With your Steam session, ForceBuy reads your Steam market history, which lists every market purchase and sale with its price. With a CSFloat API key it reads your CSFloat trades, and with your Buff163 session your Buff163 purchases and sales.

Each purchase is tied to the exact copy in your inventory. Where that is not certain, the match is marked "Ambiguous". Items with no purchase on record, such as trades and drops, get a cost by hand, from a CSV import or from a cost rule such as "drops cost nothing".

See also: How to find what you paid for your CS2 skins; How ForceBuy works.

Do I have to give ForceBuy my Steam cookie?

No. Without it ForceBuy reads the public view of your inventory, which needs your inventory to be public, and values it with current prices. Purchase prices then come from CSFloat, Buff163 or your own entries.

With the cookie ForceBuy can also read a private inventory, the contents of your storage units, your inventory history and your Steam market history.

What is the Steam cookie, and how long does it last?

It is the cookie named steamLoginSecure that your browser keeps while you are signed in to steamcommunity.com. It is your whole signed-in session, not a read-only permission, because Steam offers no such permission. You copy it from your browser's developer tools; ForceBuy shows the steps for Chrome, Edge and Firefox.

A pasted session lasts about 24 hours, then Steam ends it. Steam can also end it earlier, for example when you sign out. Nothing is lost when it ends: ForceBuy falls back to your public inventory, and the next session catches up on the history in between.

Is it allowed to store the Steam cookie in ForceBuy?

Storing the cookie in a third-party tool goes against Steam's terms of use, and Valve can restrict or ban an account for it. The same applies to the Buff163 session and Buff163's user agreement. ForceBuy only reads with them, but that does not change the rule, and ForceBuy asks you to confirm that you take this risk before it stores either.

If you do not want that risk, use the public inventory and add costs from CSFloat or by hand.

Can ForceBuy trade, sell or list my items?

No. Every request ForceBuy sends to Steam, CSFloat, Buff163 or any other marketplace is a GET request that only reads. ForceBuy never lists, buys, sells, trades or accepts an offer, not even behind a confirmation.

A Steam or Buff163 session would allow those actions to anyone who holds it. That is why stored sessions and keys are encrypted, never shown again, and every use is listed under Settings > Stored access.

Which marketplaces does ForceBuy use for prices?

Prices for the whole CS2 catalogue come from the Steam Community Market, CSFloat, Buff163, YouPin898, Skinport and C5Game and are updated several times a day. You choose which marketplace's price counts as the value of your items. Purchase and sale histories are read from the Steam Community Market, CSFloat and Buff163.

How does ForceBuy calculate value, fees and profit?

An item's value is the price a buyer pays on the marketplace you chose, with what you would keep after that marketplace's fee shown below it. Realised profit is what a sale left you after the fee, minus what the item cost.

Marketplaces report sales differently: Steam shows what you received, CSFloat and Buff163 show what the buyer paid. ForceBuy takes the fee off where it is still included, so both count the same way. A sale whose cost is not known is not counted as profit. Amounts are converted into your currency with the reference rates of the European Central Bank.

See also: Steam Market fees explained.

What does ForceBuy store, and how do I delete it?

Your SteamID64, public display name and avatar address; your ledger with items, prices, dates, notes, collections and settings; and any access you choose to store. Stored sessions and keys are encrypted and expire after 30 days without a sign-in. The server is in Germany. There is no advertising and no analytics.

You can export your holdings as CSV and delete your account with everything in it under Settings. Deletion is immediate and cannot be undone.

See also: Privacy statement.

Can other people see my inventory on ForceBuy?

No. ForceBuy has no public profile, inventory or item pages. Your ledger is shown only to you, after you sign in with your Steam account.

Who can use ForceBuy right now?

ForceBuy on the web is in a closed test. Sign in with Steam to find out whether your account is part of it. If it is not, the page shows your SteamID64 and an address to ask for access.

Without signing in, the demo shows the whole app with invented items and prices. It needs no Steam account and is deleted after two hours.

Can I run ForceBuy on my own computer?

Yes. The local app for Windows does the same as the web version, with your data, keys and Steam session in one folder on your PC. It is on the ForceBuy releases page on GitHub.
